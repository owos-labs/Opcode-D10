# Armour

Like aforementioned in the Weapons chapter, we are using a rating system that is Approximately similar to **GOST R 50744-95 (2002)** to determine the **Armour Rating (AR)** of each piece of armour.

Said pieces of armour comes with a **Coverage Area**, Armour Rating applied to said area, and an **Armour Material** matches the said armor rating.

Generally speaking, AR ranges from **0** — effectively no protection whatsoever — to around **60**, which roughly equivalent to **NIJ IV**.

Armour with higer grades, such as **NIJ IV+** or **STANAG 4569 Level III**, which in theory may stop something along the line like .50 AP, can have Armour Ratings above **70**.

Any piece of armour (including armour plates) uses the following format:

**Name / Material / Coverage (Primary) / Coverage (Secondary) / Coverage AR (Primary) / Coverage AR (Secondary)**

- **Material:** In the current version, this mostly doesn’t mean shit mechanically, but generally speaking, steel plates are heavier, ceramic plates are more brittle, while UHMWPE tends to have worse days against buffeier AP and API ammunition while weighs somewhat less. We figured this is should be pretty much remain as an RP-related thing, so for now we'll leave most of the decision-making to the GM (gm is you) and future expansion designers (said designer is you). With that being said, we do provide some basic rules further down in case you fancy something of a fair-and-balanced implementation for your game. It is but an example, and you get to do whatever you want with it.
- **Coverage (Primary):** The areas protected by plates and other hard material, if any.
- **Coverage (Secondary):** Areas protected by soft armor or filling.

In the goal of simplifing calculations, for parts when Cnverage (Primary) and Coverage (Secondary) overlap, use Coverage(Primary). In general, stacking different materials generally provides less protection than their would-be protection values added together due to deformations and all the good stuff.

For example:

A **NIJ IV plates** and **NIJ II soft armour** containing plate carrier may provide soft-armour coverage on **Upper Limbs, Upper Body,** and **Upper Legs**, with hard armour protecting the **Upper Body**. The protective material may consist of **ceramic (AR 60)**  and  **kevlar (AR 25)**, resulting in:

**Generic Heavy Vest / Ceramic, Kevlar / Upper Body / Upper Limbs, Upper Body, Lower Limbs / 65 / 25**

Generally speaking, if multiple layers of armour overlap over the same body location, add approximately **+5 to +10 AR** to the area with the highest Armour Rating if you want. If the rig provides partial protection on any limb (e.g. armguards, neck guards), apply Armour Ratings to that limb, that is, unless the attacker explicitly decides to aim for an unprotected section.

### Optional Rule: Materials

Generally speaking, modern (up to early 2026) armour plates (starts from around **AR 30**) are mostly consist of **Armor Steel(or alloy), UHMWPE, and ceramics**.

Armour steel is the heaviest while providing reliable and trustworthy consistency, albeit metal fatigue and deformations are always things.

Ceramic armour technically stops whatever projectile below its rated level without problems, but anything defeats it fucks the whole plate up from time to time, it's like throwing a snowball at a pane of glass, then later on replacing the snowball with a rock.

UHMWPE's main problem is that, it is, at the end of the day, **PE**. High temperature or high-velocity projectiles, or if you really want us to say it out loud, APIs, tend to screw it up and by a more than fair margin than almost everyone expects. 

For all facts considered, the majority of common-seen modern ballistic plates employs at least one of the aforementioned materials, we decides that in Opcode:// D10, the **Material** property represents the armour's **primary energy-absorbing material** instead of, you know.

Here is one exemplar implementation which you may expand upon.

**UHMWPE** always uses the lightest possible Weight allowed by its AR. Reduce its Weight by **one protection category (e.g. lvl4 8-12kg -> 6-8kg)**. Against projectile with Penetration equal or greater than its AR, reduce its AR by **one level (10 AR)**.

**Ceramic** reduces damage by **two damage dice per 10 points of underpenetration** instead of one whenever the attack fails to penetrate. However, upon it is penetrated, treat its Armour Rating as **1/2 of the original value** against all subsequent instances of damage.

**Steel** always uses the highest Weight category allowed by its AR specification, but suffers no additional penalties.

# Cover and Concealment

First of all, **Cover** and **Concealment** are not the same thing. We are, however, explaining both here because apparently somebody has to.

Any Cover or Concealment comes **AR** and **SSP**, or **Armour Rating** and **Structural Strength Points**. AR is a representation of the hardness of the object while SSP is a representation of the structural strength of said object. These values are given each **square metre / centimetre in thickness**. For objects below this scope, it is treated as that it met said dimensions for the ease of calculation.

It is apparent that Cover and Concealment offeres concealment therefore grants increased diffculty against aiming. We have provided a reference in this chapter.

Whenever a piece of Cover is dealt damage against and the attack would've hit a target behind it, add the Cover's **AR** to the target's Armour during damage resolution stage, then reduce the Cover's **SSP** by the damage dealt, at a rate of **1 SSP per point of damage**.

Should the body location under attack is not covered by the object, said effect does not apply.

Upon a piece of Cover losing more than **half its original SSP**, reduce its level of concealment by **one level**. It should offer visibility level no less than **Visible**.

Should layered Covers are involved, add the AR of all layers involved as bonus AR for defender and deal SSP damage against all applicable pieces of Cover during resolution.

**Concealment** ( e.g. foliage) — provides no meaningful energy absorption, therefore provides **visibility protection only**, and could offer no damage reduction.

# Combat Information Economics

In Opcode:// D10, the information used for localization during combat is divided into three levels:

**Appoximate, Exact,** and **Precise**.

At the beginning of combat, every unit within participating said event automatically acquires at least **Approximate location** on all unit involved, until their information state is updated.

### Approximate Location

**Approximate Localization** implies "The fact that this unit is present is known".

Information available under Approximate level includes:

* Whether the target is present.
* Outgoing damage dealt by said unit.
* The Initiative of said unit.

Once a Approximately localized unit performs any action sufficiently obvious to reveal its position ( for example, firing a weapon or communicating in a clearly audible fashion), its information level is upgraded to **Exact Localization** for all units aware. You cannot declare an attack against an Approximately Localized unit, you can, however, still perform attacks such as ways of dealing effective damage:

> "I'm shooting through this wall."
> "I think they're in this room. I'm nading it."

In response to actions like these, the GM should generally roll for **secret** damage and provide no more than barely meaningful feedback regarding the actual result (e.g. "The bullet went through the wall").

### Exact Localization

**Exact Localization** imples "Somebody knows this unit is roughly here, or is expecting the unit to be here through available and concrete information", for example, they heard gunfire but no footsteps afterward — but does not have direct visual contact. Declaring an attack against a target under **Exact Localization** suffers a **−5 penalty** for its respective attack roll. Starting from Exact Localization, a unit can obtain information regarding damage already dealt to the target.

### Full Localization

**Full Localization** generally means "The target is actively located through visual channel or any sensor". Declaring attack against a target under **Full Localization** suffers **no additional penalty** from localization.

# Health System

This is a health system designed for humanoid creatures. In case a unit in your story doesn’t quite come equipped with a similar set of limbs, you’ll need to make the appropriate adjustments yourself.

## Overview

Your humanoid character (in plain English: your **PC**) comes with a number of body parts. These consist of the **Head, Torso, Primary Hand, Secondary Hand, Left Leg,** and **Right Leg**. Your Primary Hand could either be your left or right hand, depending on your preference and works the same.

A character has a number of **Hit Points (HP)** equal to **(WIL × 0.5 + BOD × 2) + 6**, distributed amongst their body parts according to the table below.

When resolving damage against a unit during combat, unless the attacker has explicitly selected a location to hit, roll **1D10** to determine the hit location. Explosives always distribute their damage evenly to **every body part** of all units within their affected area.

On top of this, you also have a separate **Stun Meter** equal to your HP. Whenever you take a hit, add the damage you received to this meter.

When the HP of a body part is completely depleted, that part enters the **Destroyed** state. This means that, thanks to the damage it just took, the limb is now barely capable of functioning normally anymore — think tendon damage from a nice, pleasant comminuted fracture.

If a body part in the **Destroyed** state takes more than 5 damage after it has been destoryed, or a single attack has dealt **original hp+2** damage after it's destroyed, it gains the **Separated** state.

What does that mean? Well, that part of your body is obviously not going to take your command, regardless of whether you want it to or not — maybe blood loss caused necrosis, maybe the nerves went a bit into the strike-ier kind of lifestyle, or the entire thing just got blown up into the sky.

Despite the apparent fact we are using the term **Separated**, however, this does *not* necessarily mean the limb has literally separated from your body.

And apparently any saves caused by **Destroyed** or **Separated** effects should be made every time said limb is hit.

Whenever a **Destroyed** body part takes damage, divide that damage evenly amongst all adjacent body parts, rounding down.

## Stun Saves

A **Stun Save** is effectively anything related to shock.

Hypotension from blood loss? Stun save. Pain-induced shock? Stun save. Got yourself introduced to the business end of a Taser? Stun save.

Doesn’t really matter. You’re making a Stun Save regardless.

For Stun Save, roll:

**1D10 + WIL + Constitution vs. 10 + Stun Points**

If you fail, you suffer a penalty equal to your **margin of failure** on all checks until the end of the combat round, the end of the scene, or until the thing that caused the effect is no longer dealing with you, and this certain penalty is cumulative, you takes the sum of all margin of failures as the penalty.

Once this meter is full, you...well, **peaces out** (albeit the peace part is relatively debatable). The good news? Until somebody manages to make a successful **Stabilise Wounds** check on you, you get to get some proper sleep. For every combat turn you are not making a stun save (or in case you are out of combat, every half minute), you recovers 2 points from your stun meter and lowers 2 points of stun penalty.

## Death Saves

As the name might have already suggested, a **Death Save** generally means you’ve encountered a bit of a problem.

Whatever caused it, you make one Death Save immediately upon entering the Death Save state, and another one during every subsequent combat round. Outside combat, make one every **minute** instead.

Roll:

**1D0 + BOD + Constitution vs. 10 + Damage Taken**

If you fail, odds you are a bit dead.

But when you think about the bright side, if you succeed, you get to reduce the Difficulty of your next Death Save by **one level**. You must continue making Death Saves until its Difficulty falls below **10**, or until somebody successfully performs a **Stabilise Wounds** check on you.

Any willing unit near you can also help while you are making a Death Save. By spending either a **Full Action**, or whatever actions they have remaining during the current round, they may add their relevant **Medical Skill level** to your Death Save.

## Stabilising Wounds

You may use any applicable first-aid-related Skill — such as **Medical: First Aid** or **Knowledge: Medicine** — to provide first aid to a target other than yourself.

Doing so is a **Standard Action**.

This check may also be used to preserve a limb that has entered the **Separated** state — for example, applying enough first aid to prevent a limb from necrosis before proper medical aid arrives.

At the end of the current combat round or the scene, the limb becomes a **Destroyed** limb instead.

Attempting to **Stabilise Wounds on yourself** is a **Full Action**.

## Recovery

Any injury may be recovered from through whatever appropriate methods exist in your setting.

As a rule of thumb, a character should naturally recover around **2 Hit Points per day**, face it, this is a game afterall, so we are not bothering with the extremely long natural recover that otherwise you will surely deal with from time to time.

If a limb has been **Separated** and receives no first aid — see **Stabilising Wounds** — then, in principle, it should no longer possesses the Hit Points (or you are now one hand short in case you do fancy this phrasing).

Any attack that would've dealt damage to this body part from hit location roll does **not** deal damage to any other body part, and yes you are mostly right, even though the limb isn’t actually on you anymore, it still takes up a slot on the hit-location table.

Depending on your setting, you may provide some method of restoring Hit Points to a Separated limb and eventually restoring the limb itself.

# Optional Rules

For adjusting things to your particular and acquired taste...

Apply these rules to everyone, or only to your PCs and friendly NPCs, since who gives a shit.

## Optional Rule: Soft-Kills, Combat Incapacitation, and NPC Health

For those GMs who aren’t particularly enthusiastic about murdering your player character, we offer a **Soft Kill / Severe Injury Combat Removal** rule to you.

Whenever your character enters **Death Save**, feel free to simply consider them **out of combat and no longer a valid target**.

Then deal with all those lovely injury checks after the fight is over.

For NPCs, considering how much of a pain in the ass it would be to make saving throws for every single one of them, you may simply skip **Stun Save** entirely and declare an NPC dead immediately once they would enter **Death Save**.

## Optional Rule: Simplified Health Pool and Simplified Saves

You may instead use a much simpler health system. With this particular rule enabled, characters no longer possess separate HP pools for their body parts. A hit is only considered a **headshot** when the attack roll scores a **Critical Success**, in which case it deals **double damage**.

Characters only start to roll **Stun Saves** once each time they are hit when their current HP falls to **1/4 of their maximum HP**, and only start to roll **Death Saves** when their HP is completely depleted.

Or whatever else you want, after all Opcode:// D10 is **your** TRPG system.

## Optional Rule: Much Forgiving Hit Points

Apply a multiplier to the normal amount of HP — generally **×2 or ×3** — for GM who happens to fancy messier fights and offers PCs a little more room for tactical depth.

We do recommend using this rule with any **sword-and-sorcery-related expansion**, potentially together with the **Simplified Health** rule for a smoother narrative experience and a lighter combat workload, after all your paladin won't be happy after gets murdered by fist fights (see Combat).

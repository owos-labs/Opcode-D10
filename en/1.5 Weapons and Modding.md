In Opcode:// D10, we decided to design melee weapons, ranged weapons, and ammunition as separated entity. All fact considered, ranged weapons are normally but launchers for ammunition, whilst melee weapons — and a series of other weapons that depend on the character's physical strength to work — relies on the character that is using said weapons.

## Ranged Weapons

### Projectile Weapons

Ranged ballistic weapon uses the following template:

**Weapon Name / Calibre / Accuracy / Reliability / ROF / Fire Modes / Range / Concealability**

Every ranged ballistic weapon has a calibre, and can only use ammunition compatible with the calibre.

For example:

> AEK-971 (KORD 6P67) / 5.45x39mm / +0 / N / 900 / SA,FA / 400m / P  
> Kriss Vector-9 / 9mm / -1 / N / 1200 / SA,FA,B 2 / 100m / C

Which means the AEK-971 is a weapon chambered for 5.45x39mm ammunition, with Normal Accuracy, Normal Reliability, a rate of fire of 900 rounds per minute, and is hard to conceal.

**Accuracy**  
The bonus the weapon provides to its Marksmanship roll.

**Reliability**  
The weapon's chance to malfunction on a Critical Failure during a Marksmanship roll:

**1D10 < [Unreliable (U): 2 | Normal (N): 1 | Very Reliable (V): does not malfunction]**

**Concealability**  
A rough measurement of exactly how well the weapon can be hidden under different circumstances. This includes:

- **E (Excellent):** Compact handguns such as the compact Glock series and PPK.
- **G (Good):** An AKS-74U with the stock folded, no additional accessories and no magazine inserted; an MPX with a 4.5" barrel and folded stock; a .357 revolver.
- **C (Common):** An AKS-74U with a magazine inserted; AR-platform pistols.
- **P (Poor):** A 10.5" AR, AK-100 series rifle with integral front sight, or an AK-platform weapon with a folding stock.
- **N (Non-Concealable):** Any AR with a barrel longer than 10.5", or any full-size AK platform.

### Ammunition

In Opcode:// D10, ranged projectile weapon in itself generally does not deal damage — that is, except that case you have decided to use it as melee weapon. They need ammunition to deal damage.

Different calibre categories employs different damage dice. For Penetration, we use a numerical system vaguely similar to **GOST R 50744-95 (2002)**, then multiply our estimate of the ammunition's actual penetration capability by 10.

Ammunition uses the following format:

**Name / Calibre / Damage / Penetration**

For example:

> 5.45x39mm 7N6 (PS) / 5.45x39mm / 3d6 / 30  
> 5.45x39mm 7N39 (PP BS) "Needle Box" / 5.45x39mm / 3d6-2 / 55  
> 9x19mm RIP / 9mm Para / 4d4 / 9  
> 9x19mm FMJ / 9mm Para / 3d4 / 15

Generally speaking, different calibre classes use different damage dice:

- **Pistol calibres** use **d4** damage dice and generally have **0–20 Penetration**.
- **Intermediate rifle calibres** use **d6** damage dice and generally have **20–40 Penetration**.
- **Full-length rifle calibres** use **d6** damage dice with a modifier and generally have **25–60 Penetration**.
- **Anti-materiel calibres** — starting at around **12mm** — use **D10** damage dice. Penetration generally starts at **50**.
- **Artillery calibres** — starting at around **37mm** — use **D10 or D20** damage dice and generally have at least **100 Penetration**.

**Shotgun Ammunition**

For shotgun ammunition, Opcode:// uses a dedicated damage model.

Shotgun ammunition ccomes with a new statistic: **Projectile Count**, representing the number of effective projectiles used for damage calculation.

The format is:

**Name / Calibre / Damage / Penetration / Effective Projectile Count**

For **Slugs, Flechettes, and AP Sabot rounds**, calculate damage normally and double the Range of the weapon firing them.

For **Buckshots**, resolve attacks normally against targets within **1/2 Range**. For the following examples, we'll be using **12GA #00 (8.4mm) Buckshot**.

Starting beyond **1× Range**, reduce the number of damage dice by 1/4 (**6d3**), roll for two hit locations, reduce the hit Difficulty by **2**, and apply half the original Penetration and half the original damage to each of those locations. Do the same thing to targets within **0.5m** range of the original target.

Starting beyond **1 1/4× (1.25×) Range**, reduce the number of damage dice by another 1/4 (**4d3**), reduce the hit Difficulty by **5**, roll three hit locations, and apply 1/3 of the original Penetration and 1/3 of the damage to each. Do the same thing to targets within **0.9m** range of the original target.

Starting beyond **1 1/2× (1.5×) Range**, reduce the hit Difficulty by **7**, reduce the damage dice to **2d3**, and distribute **1 damage to every body part**, up to the ammunition's Projectile Count. Do the same thing to targets within **1.3m**.

Beyond that, the attack deals the minimum possible damage to one body part — for 12GA #00, for example, **min(1d2) = 1** — reduce the hit Difficulty by **10**, and do the same thing to targets within **1.5m**.

Beyond **2× Range**, no matter what the original damage was, it deals **no damage**.

For all sakes out there nobody actually wants to count how many pellets are in a shell of 12GA birdshot, so we've simplified this part a little.

**12GA Birdshot**, regardless of its actual shot size, deals **2D10 damage within 1m**, **1d10-5 outside 1m**, and **1d10 within 5m**.

**20GA Birdshot** deals **1D10**, **1D10-3**, and **1D10-5** damage respectively, then deals **1 damage beyond 5m**, and no damage at all beyond **7m**.

Birdshot always has **1 Penetration**.

A few common shotgun loads are provided below:

> 12GA RIP Slug / 12GA / 6d6 / 15  
> 12GA Lead Slug / 12GA / 4d6 / 25 / 1  
> 12GA AP Sabot / 12GA / 2d6+1 / 35  
> 12GA #0 (9.1mm) Buckshot / 12GA / 4d6 / 15 / 6  
> 12GA #00 (8.4mm), #000 (8.1mm) Buckshot / 12GA / 8d3 / 7 / 8  
> 12GA #1 (7.6mm) Buckshot / 12GA / 6d4 / 9 / 8  
> 12GA #4 (6.1mm) Buckshot / 12GA / 8d3-2 / 13 / 9  
> 20GA #1 (7.6mm) Buckshot / 20GA / 5d4 / 7 / 8  
> 20GA #3 (6.1mm) Buckshot / 20GA / 6d3 / 5 / 8

### Optional: Faster Buckshot

Just in case you feel like calculating the damage of slugs is too complex, ignore every ammunition statistic except Penetration and just roll:

- **20GA:** 3d6
- **12GA:** 4d6
- **10GA:** 5D6
- **4GA:** 4d10+2

At **1/2×, 1×, 1 1/2×**, and beyond **2× Range**, apply the usual range brackets and deal no damage beyond 2× Range.

Distribute damage evenly to all targets within:

- **0.5m (1×)**
- **1m (1.5×)**
- **2m (2×)**

If there is only one target, starting at **0.5× effective Range**, remove one damage die for every additional **0.5× effective Range**, Problem solved, I hope that's simple enough.

### Optional: Cover, Non-Penetration, and Overpenetration

Different kinds of cover can stop different kinds of ranged weapon attacks.

Projectiles generally damages soft target through the temporal cavity produced by kinetic-energy transfer, which implies a projectile only achieves optimal terminal effect should it penetrate optimal amount of armors by design of said projectile.

Before resolving Armour, compare the final Penetration result post resolution.

For every **10 points of overpenetration**, reduce the final damage by **one damage dice**.

For example, a **5.56x45mm M855A1** projectile with **45 Penetration** hits a target wearing **NIJ IIIA armour (30 AR)** results in **15 net overpenetration**, should the shot deals **3D6+1** in nominal conditions, it deals **2D6+1**.

Regardless the amount overpenetration occurs, a shot always retains at least **half of its original damage dice**, rounded up.

If an attack fails to penetrate, it deals only **1/4 of its normal damage dice**, with modifiers applied separately (For example, a non-penetrating **5d6+2** attack deals **1d3+2** damage). Damage cannot be reduced below **1**, and an non-penetrating round does never triggers **Destroyed** state — see the Health chapter.

Depending on the material, the result may be one of:

- **Completely stop** the projectile, if the projectile's Penetration is below the cover's AR.
- **Fails to stop but still prevent penetration**, if the cover's AR is below the ammunition's Penetration but the SSP is below its maximum possible damage.
- **Penetration**.

If the projectile is stopped without penetration, the projectile deals its damage to the cover instead.

If the cover is penetrated, subtract the cover's AR from the projectile's Penetration, round the remaining Penetration down to the nearest ten, and reduce the projectile's damage dice according to the cover type's SSP, for every 10 ssp, one damage dice is subtracted.

Here's an example:

> A .338 Lapua Magnum fired from an AW platform deals **6D6-2 damage** and has **72 Penetration**.  
> It passes through a **20cm concrete wall (50 AR, SSP 20)**, leaving it with **20 Penetration**.  
> The target is unarmoured (**AR = 0**), so the shot also has **20 points of net overpenetration**.  
> The ammunition originally deals **6D6-2** damage. Removing four damage dice — two from 20 points of overpenetration and two from 20 points of cover — leaves **2D6-2 damage**.  
> The roll comes out to **4 damage**.  
> The round leaves a fairly disgusting through-and-through wound, but the lucky bastard on the receiving end is still very much alive and, having now figured out the apparent fact that there's a sniper in town, begins working on their very own counter sniper campaign.

Should a projectile fails to penetrate, roll for damage, deal **half the damage** to the cover.

Starting with **anti-materiel calibres**, overpenetration no longer reduces damage due to their muzzle energy.

Starting with **artillery calibres**, cover which AR is lower than **the calibre of armour-piercing ammunition × 10** — for example, 370AR for 37mm — does not inflict cover-related Penetration penalties if the projectile is able to achieve initial penetration.


## Explosives

> "Lemmetellya kiddo, this ain't no FPS. The best way to do CQC is don't be there."

Explosives always ignore Armour and deal full damage to targets within their affected area.

Different types of explosives — such as **HE, Fragmentation,** and **Thermal** — resolve damage differently.

Should the payload be fired through a projectile weapon, for example, **40mm M441 HE fired from M203 40mm UGBL** — make the attack using **Marksmanship**.

For hand grenades, use **Athletics / Throwing**.

Should your target or the impact point of the grenade within the explosive's **Fuse Distance**, any unit inside the damage radius may make a **Reflex Save** against by your throwing successes to attempt on throwing it back. If they do, resolve their throwing attempt normally.

Explosives use the following format:

**Name / Lethal Radius / Secondary Damage Radius / Type (HE: High Explosive, F: Fragmentation, T: Thermal) / Launch Type (B: Ballistic, T: Thrown) / Damage / Duration / Fuse Distance**

The secondary damage radius begins at the edge of the lethal radius.

Examples:

> 40x46mm M441 / 2m / 2.5m / F / B / 2D6 / 0 / 15m  
> RG-60 / 3m / 0.5m / HE / T / 3D6 / 0 / 5m  
> F-1 / 1m / 3.5m / F / T / 2D6 / 0 / 5m

Every unit within the affected area should make a **Reflex Save**.

On a success, reduce the damage tier by one level:

**Lethal -> Secondary -> No Damage**

This represents the target noticed things will happen and decided they have better places to be.

### HE

The category of explosive deals damage through overpressure and shock wave — for example, **RG-60 thermobar** or nitroglycerine. Hello average nitroglycerine lovers in the Dark Future.

HE explosives have a **Lethal Radius** and a **Secondary Damage Radius** produced by overpressure and shock wave.

Soft target within lethal radius is killed regardless whether they are armoured, and within secondary damange radius, the explosive deals damage as stat specified.

These warhead does not damage targets behind sealed area and/or enclosed barricades, but will destroy any said barricades whose AR is lower than the explosive's maximum possible damage (e.g. for 2d6, 12, 10d10+50, 150) within their Lethal Range.

### Fragmentation

The category of explosives that damage through overpressure and fragments. They have a **Lethal Radius** through overpressure and a secondary **Secondary Damage Radius** through fragmentation.

Targets within Secondary Damage Radius must make a **Luck Save** or take the damage as stat specified.

These explosives do not damage targets behind sealed walls should the target is within Lethal Radius, and do not damage target behind enclosed walls should the target is within fragmentation radius.

The explosive will damage any wall inside of the Lethal Radius, should the target's AR is lower than the explosive's maximum possible damage.

### Thermal

These explosives — such as thermite, white phosphorus, and incendiaries — deal continuous damage by contaminating the target through incendiary ingredients contained.

If a target fails a **Reflex Save vs. the thrower's attack roll**, for every round starts from this round, the explosive deals its damage as stat specified.

Its **Lethal Radius** represents the range within which contamination is guaranteed. Should the target is contaminated, they are able to make a check through a Full-Full Action for removal.

Targets within the Secondary Damage Radius take one instance of damage, should they are inside of said radius.

Most artillery-calibre weapons combine the damage effects of both **Explosives** and **Projectile Weapons**.

Here's an example:

> The defenders and attackers are locked in a stalemate inside a narrow corridor with equal manpower.  
> The attacker's breacher pulls an RG-40 from his vest, shouts **"SURPRISE, MOTHERFUCKER"**. The grenade draws a parabola down the hallway as the breacher throws it away.  
> The attack roll comes with **20** as result. Two defenders are inside the Lethal Distance and one is inside the Wounding Distance.  
> All three defenders make Reflex Saves: **REF + Athletics + 1D10**, rolling **21, 19, 21**. All scatters while one of them fails to get clear of the lethal radius.  
> Defenders outside of the Lethal Distance takes **8 damage**, distributed proportionally over the whole body, survives through **1 damage** on all body parts.  
> Of the three defenders, one is now Cargo 200, one is Cargo 300, and the other one draws his M67 with their shaking hands.

### Other Throwables

Other throwables, in general, includes things such as **smoke grenades, stun / concussion grenades, IR-enhancers, flares,** and **glow sticks**.

These generally require either a **Move Action** or a **Standard Action** to throw.

Starting from the round which after a smoke grenade is thrown, it provides varying levels of concealment around its landing point smoke generally lasts for at least **two minutes** indoors and *60 seconds* outdoors. Depending on wind direction and wind speed the throwable may provide:

- **1/3-height concealment** over an area up to **20m**
- **1/2-height concealment** over an area up to **7.5m**
- **Full concealment** within **2.5m**

Smoke grenade generally provides **full-body concealment** indoors and blocks vision, effectively reducing localization accuracy by **two levels**.

This concealment applies whenever a unit is targeting any unit opposite of the smoke, always applies to units inside the smoke, and units inside the smoke must also apply the same concealment modifier when targeting units on either side.

In an open or semi-ope environment, smoke can be dispersed through a **High Explosive** or **Fragmentation** explosive detonating within its affected area.

In Opcode:// D10, we leave the actual weapon modification and the larp to the players and only provide a series of template examples.

In general, the majority of weapon comes with:

- **1 Optic Slot**
- **1 Secondary Optic Slot**
- Up to **3 Accessory Slots**
- **1 Muzzle Slot**
- **1 Stock Slot**
- **1 Receiver Slot**
- **1 Barrel Slot**
- **1 Magazine Slot**

We encourage GMs to write their own modification rules for their players' weapons.

### Receiver

The Receiver slots represents modifications to the receiver assembly.

This generally includes uppers which usually provide Accuracy and Reliability bonuses — modernization or integral conversions, which nominally providing Accuracy and/or additional slots, and / or whatever else (e.g. smartgun conversion, in case you are from a futuristic world setting or calibre swaps, swapping the dust cover for your AK patterned rifle is also included within this category).

### Accessories

 Lasers, flashlights, rangefinders and all sorts of similar accessories goes here.

Rail covers, in case if you really want them, offers decorative value and the lolz, extra rails if you fancy larping, or front grips.

### Optics

Gain additional Range or Accuracy at the cost of reduced Range and Accuracy outside the optic's designated zoom interval.

### Secondary Optics

Mount a secondary optic to ignore the penalties of a high-magnification primary optic if you have one.
Switching between them requires at least a **Move Action**.

### Muzzle

Muzzle devices - cans, brakes, generally modify the weapon's Accuracy bonus.

Suppressors generally reduce Concealability. As a common sense, a suppressed .22LR (which offers effectively the smallest report profile), emits around 100db upon each firing. 

### Stock

Make the gun look better, or gain extra Concealability through removing the stock and make the weapon less stable.

### Barrel

Trade Concealability and Reliability for additional Range and Accuracy, or vice versa. 

In addition, you may install a larger magazine at the cost of Concealability.

## Melee Weapons

Melee weapons, steel goes in, blood comes out, relatively straightforward.

Melee weapons use the following template:

**Weapon Name / Damage / Compatible Skills / Attributes Required / Penetration / Minimum BOD / Concealability / Additional Actions (0)**

Keep in mind that many weapons employs unique Skills. Katana may use **Kenjutsu**, machete takes **Bladed Weapons**, while a western sword you want **Two-Handed Sword** Skill.

All melee weapons deal a specific amount of damage, a few templates are provided below.

A weapon's size determines the size its damage dice — generally **D4, D6,** or **D8**, while the mass determines the count of damage dice, improvised melee weapons follow the same limitations.

> Dagger (and small bladed weapons) / 2d4 / Bladed Weapons, Brawling, Martial Arts / REF, BOD / 10 / 2 / E / 0  
> Kukri (and small-medium bladed weapons) / 3d4 / Bladed Weapons, Survival / REF, BOD / 15 / 3 / G / 0  
> Broadsword (and medium bladed weapons) / 2d6 / Bladed Weapons / 15 / REF / 3 / G / 0  
> Two-Handed Sword (and large bladed weapons) / 3d6 / Swordsmanship / BOD / 20 / 4 / N / 0
> Chainsaw / 2d8 / Bladed Weapons, Engineering, Maintenance / BOD / 50 / 3 / N / 0

Some (actually most of the) weapons can be used with different Skills, regardless the skill you have, upon an attack you shall only use **one Skill and its relevant Specialisation**.

For melee combat, roll for attack with:

**Weapon Key Attribute + Weapon Skill + Specialisation + Weapon Damage vs. Defence Key Attribute + Defence Key Skill + Specialisation + 1D10**

The attacker and defender must each declare one of the following position upon attack declaration:

**Head / Upper Body / Lower Body**

If both choose the same position, the defender gains an additional **1D10** on defensive roll.

If the attacker deals positive damage, they may apply that damage to the reasonable hit location that belongs to the location they declared upon attack declaration or roll for **1D10** in order to determine the hit location as normal.

In damage resolution, compare Penetration and Armour. If the Armour covering the target area is higher than the weapon's Penetration, the attack deals **no damage**, otherwise, the attack deals damage equal to the attacker's **net success**.

If the attacker rolls a **Critical Failure**, the defender may immediately make an attack roll.

We encourage designer for the actual weapon and martial art Skills to provide melee weapons with free **Additional Actions**.

Unarmed attacks do not compare Penetration against Armour AR normally, they represent grappling, locking skills, restraints, strangulations, attacking gaps in armour that is intended to bypass hard protection. Unarmred attacks ignores armor. The attacker deals **1D4 damage**, plus another **1D4 for every 2 points of BOD above 5**, rounds up.

Here's an example:

> Cornered, the defender draws the Safari on the belt (**S〇g Safari 13" / 2d6+2 / Bladed Weapons / 20 / BODY / 4 / G / 0**) decides to make a last stand in the corner of the corridor.  
> The attacker nods，drops the dried rifle in hands with no ammo left, reaches into the plate carrier(**NIJ III+, Coverage: BODY, AR 45**) , blade out (**Ka-Bar M9 / 2d4 / Bladed Weapons, Brawling, Martial Arts / REF, BOD / 10 / 2 / E / 0**).  
> Eyes meet. Wind gust. Ka-Ching. Defender makes move (**20 Initiative**). While the attacker, not expecting the strike after a ten-minute chase, was half a beat behind(**14 Initiative**).  
> The defender strikes. Attacker's shoulder (**11 vs. 20**). Instant.  
> The attacker's arm raised. Body steady. Doges to the side, with his momentum transforms into a lethal stab towards the defender's waist (**15 vs. 19**). Too slow. Just a fraction.  
> The defender turns the blade towards the attacker's unarmoured neck — **23 vs. 19, 4 damage**. Blade soaked in blood. Body drops.  
> The defender wipes the knife clean, then, as adrenaline finally crashes, dropped down on the ground next to the pond of blood gushing out of the attacker.
> Room silent. The only thing left to hear is traffic from the street beyond the corridor outside beyond the defender's pounding heart.

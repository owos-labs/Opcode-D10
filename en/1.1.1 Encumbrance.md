This is an optional detailed encumbrance rule. Albeit, in principle, a character get to carry as much as the story allows, we decided to provide a somewhat workable-with way to deal with it.

In Opcode:// D10, encumbrance is separated into **Weight Tolerance** and **Carryable Weight**.

The following sections explain how to calculate these attributes.

## Weight Tolerance (WGT)

Weight Tolerance is the amount of weight a character’s body can carry before the load leads to mechanical injury. It affects the character’s movement speed and might impose penalties in certain scenarios.

A character can carry up to **20 + 10 × BOD** kilograms.

Upon the character’s current encumbrance reaches **1/2** of WGT, their movement speed is reduced to **3/4**.

Upon the character’s current encumbrance reaches **3/4** of WGT, they suffer a **−1 REF** penalty, and their movement speed is reduced to **1/2**.

When the character’s current encumbrance exceeds their Weight Tolerance, the load prevents them from walking normally. From this point and onward, they would need a **Fortitude Save** to avoid the load from injuring their body. Each additional **5 kg** increases the difficulty by one level. Upon a failure, each lower limb takes **1d4 damage**, representing a possible fracture, contusion, or sprain.

In this case, the character’s movement speed is reduced to **1/4**. Make a said save per combat round, or every minute outside of combat.

## Carryable Weight

Carryable Weight is the maximum weight that the character’s equipment and clothing can carry in their current state.

As a rule of thumb, while wearing ordinary clothing, a character can carry up to **3 kg** of loose items, such as a phone, wallet, or whatever else they have in pocket. In  addition to that, their hands can carry either one item weighing up to **BOD × 10 kg**, or two items weighing up to **BOD × 5 kg** each.

Backpacks and other carrying equipment can increase this amount.

# 1 Character Attributes

Opcode utilises a character creation system similar to Interlock (from R. Talsorian’s D10 game system, the predecessor to Fuzion).

In Opcode, upon character creation, no attribute should be lower than 2, nor should any be higher than 10. The number 10 is regarded as the human physical limit without any enhancements or aids, whilst 5 represents the average. 

Depending on the story design, you typically have around 50 attribute points to allocate freely; under the base rules (without expansions), you use approximately 35 attribute points to purchase your character’s attributes. 

We do not recommend that your character’s attributes exceed 8 when creating the character sheet. Character attributes are divided into three main categories: Mental, Physical and Special. The following includes a series of sub-attributes that generally apply in all circumstances:

## 1.1 Mental Attributes

This series of attributes represents a character’s mental capabilities. They include:

### REF Reflex

This attribute measures a character’s physical reaction speed and hand-eye coordination. It is applied when calculating initiative (round order). 

Furthermore, it serves as the attribute for the vast majority of attack rolls. Its derivatives include movement speed, jumping distance, and—through initiative, which dictates the maximum number of actions a character can actually perform per turn.

### INT Intelligence

This attribute measures a character’s speed of reasoning. It is used as the key attribute for the vast majority of complex mental activities and for some survival-related attribute checks.

### WIL Willpower

This attribute measures a character’s ability to react under pressure (and how steady their trigger finger would be when the adrenaline hits). It is applied to Initiative and a range of saving rolls. It is used to make Perception saves.

### CHR Charisma

The extent to which a character can draw upon their personal charm. In Opcode, we figured decoupling the character’s appearance from their Charisma (albeit generally speaking a fair looking character is unlikely to have a low Charisma attribute) is the better move. It is used in a range of social checks.

## 1.2 Physical Attributes

This set of attributes relates more to a character’s physical strength. It includes:

### BOD Constitution

A measure of a character’s physical robustness. This affects a character’s ability to recover from injury, the damage dealt when using melee weapons and certain ranged weapons, aaaaaand damage reduction. Generally, characters with a higher BOD attribute would be mostly more robust. It provides bonus to a series of derived attributes and is related to death saves.

## 1.3 Derived and Special Attributes

### LUK Luck

This is a tool-esque attribute. It is used for Luck saves and is used as a resource for rerolling certain checks. All characters come with a Luck pool, the maximum value of which is set upon character creation. This resource is reset at the end of each chapter of the story per GM's discretion. Upon making a Luck saving throw, you use your adjusted Luck value (for example, if your character has 7 Luck but burned one point of Luck for rerolling the previous check, you receive only a +6 bonus for this Luck save).

### Other

If your rules or the expansion you are using for your campaign contain other similar attributes, jam them in into their respective relevant section.

### Derived Attributes

Your character possesses a series of derived attributes:

**MOV Movement Speed** This attribute equals **(BOD + REF) × 2**, measured in metres. For sprinting at full speed without any modifiers, you may move up to MOV metres per combat round; otherwise, your movement speed is up to MOV × 0.5 metres per combat round.

**SENS Perception Range (A: Auditory / V: Visual / S: Sense)** This attribute equals to your (REF + WIL) × 2, measured in metres. This will be mostly omitted for narrative purposes, but you surely are supposed to use it for fog of war designs and decisions during combat rounds. In the absence of cover, you can hear sounds from up to {SENS} metres. In open terrain, you have a line of sight of SENS × 10 metres. This is considered the maximum distance at which you can make out certain details (read: gear) of a target. Your sensory abilities allow for a passive perception (or sixth sense or whatever they are calling it, not entirely sure) range of up to SENS × 0.5 metres.

**HP (Hit Points)** This attribute is used to indicate a character’s health status. We will describe Opcode’s life system in detail later on.

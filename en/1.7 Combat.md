# Combat Rounds

In Opcode:// D10, combat system employs a mechanism somewhat similar to **FNFF from Interlock**. Similar pretty much every TRPG in existence, everyone rolls Initiatives first, ordered by Initiatives, and proceeds per that order.

### Ambush

Should a character decides to conduct an ambush, the prerequisite is that the character themselves is **outside of the target's awareness range**, whether through positioning, concealment, or stealth. Through declaring an Ambush, the ambusher may, if the conditions allow, gain **1 additional combat round**, or receive **one additional Action during their first round**.

For an Ambush, any melee attack performed by the ambusher as the first action during the first round **cannot be defended against**, unless the attacker's roll results with a **Critical Failure**. during the Ambush Round, until the ambusher performs something that is going to make the other guy to notice them (e.g. firing a gun) the ambusher may perform, in theory, **unlimited number of Actions within their Ambush Window**.

When the ambusher begins an action that reveals their presence, the ambushed target should make a Perception Save:

**INT + Perception: Reconnaissance + 1D10 vs. 15, or the ambusher's preparation roll, whichever is higher**

This is an attempt to discover the attacker before the ambush starts, on a success, the target may defend against the first melee attack, but ranged attacks is treated as usual. *You know early in WW1 they still uses bayonet charge a lot, but see how that goes.*

### Initiative

Once the Ambush Round ends, or upon the combat turn starts, everyone rolls Initiative:

**1D10 + REF + any applicable modifiers**

Sort the results from highest to lowest.

At **REF ≥ 8, REF ≥ 10, and REF ≥ 15**, the character gains an additional **1D10 Initiative**.

In Opcode:// D10, one combat round lasts **three seconds**, any action which very obviously takes longer than three seconds should be split across two or more rounds.

## Action Queue

Anything you do in a Combat Round fundamentally operates through Actions. There are three kinds of actions:, **Movement Actions, Free Actions,** and **Standard Actions**.

### Movement Actions

One **Movement Action** is provided for free each round.

You may use a Movement Action to move up to **MOV × 0.5 metres**, or spend the whole round's movement capability to sprint for up to **MOV metres**. The maximum amount you may move in a round in any way is **MOV × 1.5 metres**. If you move the full **MOV × 1.5 metres**, you may perform no other actions that round except **Free Actions**.

By spending one entire Movement Action, you may enter or leave a Cover next to you.

You may also spend a full round's Movement Action to declare **Aim**, gaining **+1 on your next attack roll in this round**. For every additional round spent aiming, gain another **+1**, up to no more than **half of the relevant Skill level**.

If you perform another Movement Action other than aiming — or shift your aim to another target — you need to start the aiming process again. Narratively, this represents things such as **walking your fire**, or simply holding your breath and waiting for the right moment.

You may also spend a Movement Action to retrieve an item from your carrying equipment or pockets.

### Free Actions

**Free Actions** are things that take practically no time and can generally happen alongside other actions.

This includes, but is not limited to:

* Communication.
* Simple checks.
* Blink.
* Identifying target weaknesses, equipment, and weapons.
* Other similarly trivial things.

### Standard Actions

**Standard Actions**, or simply **Actions**, are those which actually demand time or attention.

Examples include:

* Firing a weapon.
* Attacking.
* Pressing a button or operating complex switch.
* Reloading.
* Attempting to clear a malfunction.
* Throwing a grenade.
* Pulling something out of a backpack.
* Attempting combat first aid.

Your character has an **Action Queue**, and may perform multiple Standard Actions should their Initiative allows. 

Initiative determines the maximum number of Actions you may perform in that round. At the beginning of each round, you should first declare how many Standard Actions you intend to perform.

Every Standard Action beyond the first requires a cumulatively increasing **3 Initiative**. For example, with **21 Initiative**:

* First Action costs **3 Initiative**.
* Second Action costs **3 + 3 = 6 Initiative**.
* Third Action costs **3 + 3 + 3 = 9 Initiative**.
* et cetra.

If you have **23 Initiative**, you may therefore perform no more than **three Actions**, since a fourth Action would require **30 Initiative**.

If you only perform **one Standard Action** during the round, there is no penalty. If you perform **two Standard Actions**, every Standard Action you make during that round suffers **−3**. A third Standard Action changes this to **−6**, applied on all Standard Actions, another one makes it **−9**, and so on.

Keep in mind that this is a **Unified Penalty** that applies to every Standard Action performed during that round.

If all three Actions during the round are identical (for example, three consecutive shooting Actions through the same weapon without reload) then the first Action suffers no penalty, the second suffers **−3**, and the third suffers **−6**. Narratively, imagine you just finished firing, immediately started fumbling around for a reload, then spend some more of your already rather precious time getting your sights trained onto the target again.

Under special circumstances (in majority of the cases, when your GM hands you an Action) you may receive specific additional Actions. Unless separately stated, these granted Actions do **not** suffer the normal Action penalty.

A Standard Action can be downgraded into a Movement Action for anything other than movement. Narratively, Movement Actions occur at the same time as other Actions. A combat round consisting of firing and movement can therefore simply represent the character **shooting while moving**.

### Suppressive Fire

Suppressive Fire is treated as a **Delayed Action**, and begins once its condition is satisfied.

For example, if you are suppressing enemies currently visible to you, then congratulations, the condition is already satisfied and you can start immediately. To perform Suppressive Fire, you need a ranged weapon capable of at least **semi-automatic fire**.

Examples include:

* Pistols.
* Crossbows.
* Assault rifles.
* Machine guns.
* Autocannons.

Roll a **Marksmanship**, and consume **all of your ROF allocation for the round**, which is mostly **1/20 of the weapon's listed ROF**. Suppressive Fire begins during the current round and lasts for at least one round. Every following round consumes ROF as usual until you explicitly ends supressive fire. You may move while performing Suppressive Fire.

Every target inside of the designated area you choose must make a **Reflex Save** upon Suppressive Fire begins, then again at the beginning of every subsequent round.

Once a unit gets a success on this Save, they don't need to make additional Saves until Suppressive Fire ends, unless they perform another Movement Action. The Difficulty of this Reflex Save equals the attacker's Suppressive Fire roll.

On failure, deal **1d(ROF / Number of Targets)** instances of damage, capped by the attacker's **margin of success** against the original attack Difficulty. 

For example, assume the attacker's weapon has a per-round ROF of **20**, the target is at Medium Range (**Difficulty 15**) and the attacker's result is **16**. Against one target, that target suffers **1D20, maximum 1 hit**, since the attacker only comes with 1 net success, while against two targets, each suffers **1D10, maximum 1 hit**. Round down when calculating the amount of ammunition available per target.

Thereafter, if there are any additional targets enter the Suppressive Fire area during their Actions, they roll as normal, except ammunition already expended against earlier targets is removed from the remaining pool. For example, if the first of two targets suffers **1D10 = 2 hits** from ten rounds allocated against them, the second target now suffers **1D8** instead, because two rounds have already found their new permanent residence inside the first target.

### Ranged Attacks

A Ranged Attack is a **Standard Action**.

The attacker first rolls:

**1D10 + Key Attribute + Key Skill + Specialisation vs. fixed Difficulty**

The fixed Difficulty is generally somewhere around **15**.

On success, roll damage according to the **Weapons and Modifications** chapter.

For Automatic Fire, you may fire up to:

**ROF / 60 rounds per Action**

Every additional round after the first suffers a penalty of:

**3 − Weapon Accuracy, minimum 0**

Starting from your **second Standard Action**, this penalty becomes:

**2 − Weapon Accuracy, minimum 0**

Starting from your **third Standard Action**, it becomes:

**1 − Weapon Accuracy, minimum 0**

This is considered **Walking Your Fire**.

If your Automatic Fire continues into any Action of the following round — and obviously you generally cannot magically manifest more ammunition from an empty magazine, so congratulations to all machine-gun and drum-magazine enthusiasts — that Action no longer suffers this penalty.

This is why snipers exist.

Yes, we're talking about you, little machine-gunner.

During a single round, you may perform Ranged Attacks using up to **three different weapons**.

Each weapon may fire at most:

**ROF / 20 rounds per round**

For Automatic Fire, you may directly determine which **X-th projectile** is the first one to miss and then resolve the applicable damage instances.

For example:

You perform a **20-round Automatic Fire attack** using a weapon with **−2 Accuracy**.

Your attack result is **20**, against **Difficulty 15** at Medium Range.

* First round hits: **20 vs. 15**
* Second round hits: **15 vs. 15**
* Third round misses: **10 vs. 15**

Resolve the resulting damage accordingly.

For **Semi-Automatic Fire**, fire one projectile, make one attack roll, and resolve one instance of damage.

For **Burst Fire**, make one attack roll and resolve multiple damage rolls according to the burst.

For bows and similar weapons, you must spend an additional **Movement Action** before every shot to prepare the weapon for firing.

That Movement Action does not necessarily need to occur during the same round.

If you are using a dice bot or plain paper, we also provide an alternative Automatic Fire system:

**First Miss.**

An Automatic Fire Action continues until your first failed attack roll.

After every successful hit, make another roll.

Keep doing this until the first failure.

Narratively, this represents you walking your shots until something goes off target, then taking a moment to breathe, stabilise the sights — scope, irons, whatever the fuck is attached to the thing — and beginning again.

**Cover and target-size modifiers do not stack. Apply only one.**

| Type                     | Difficulty Modifier | Description                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| **Cover: 1/3 Height**    |                  +2 | Attempting to hit a target behind roughly 1/3-height cover, such as sandbags.                                   |

### Throwing

Throwing is a **Standard Action**.

The attacker rolls:

**1D10 + REF + Athletics + relevant weapon Specialisation, if any vs. fixed Difficulty**

Use the Difficulty reference table above.

On success, resolve the thrown object's effect normally.

On failure, the **margin of failure determines the distance in metres by which the throw deviates**.

### Melee Attacks

See the **Weapons and Modifications** chapter.

### Reloading

If a weapon is fed using a **magazine, drum, belt, or equivalent detachable feeding device** — generally something with at least ten rounds of capacity that can be individually removed — the Reload Action **cannot be the final Action in your queue**.

For weapons using stripper clips, shotguns, or lever-action rifles, you may use your Movement Action for the round to reload.

If you are using a manually loaded weapon — such as a break-action or single-shot weapon — spend **one Movement Action and one Action** during the round to reload and prepare the weapon for firing.

If you are using a sniper rifle or pump-action shotgun, you may cycle the action using the **final available Action of the round, including a Free Action**, or using a Movement Action during the round.

### Battlefield First Aid — Stabilise Wounds

By spending the entire round, you may attempt to stabilise a target currently making Death Saves.

This may represent:

* Haemostatic powder.
* Rapid bandaging.
* Morphine.
* Adrenaline.
* Several of the above, because apparently things have gone sufficiently badly.

Roll:

**1D10 + Key Attribute + Key Skill + Specialisation vs. Difficulty (WIP)**

On success, the target is considered **Stabilised**.

You may use this on yourself or another target.

You may also use Stabilise Wounds to downgrade a **Separated Limb** effect suffered during the current round.

On failure, the target continues making Death Saves normally.

On a **Critical Failure**, you'd better hope you have some Luck left.

For a character currently making Death Saves, a Critical Failure means that, for whatever magnificent reason, you somehow managed to make the injury **worse**, and the target is now looking considerably less compatible with continued existence.

### Operate

Depending on the task, **Operate** requires either one Action or an entire round.

Pressing a button takes an Action.

Operating a switch takes an Action.

Driving and operating complex equipment generally requires the **entire round**.

## Special: Delayed Actions

You may delay your Standard Action until after a particular event, or into another unit's turn.

However, once you declare a Delayed Action, you may perform **no other Standard Actions during that round**.

## Special: Conditional Actions

You must spend one **unpenalised Standard Action** to declare a Conditional Action.

For example:

> "I shoot after the target moves."

If you declare a Conditional Action, you may **not use a Movement Action during that round**.

## Special: Full Defence

To declare **Full Defence**, spend:

* One **unpenalised Standard Action**.
* Your **final Standard Action**.
* One **Movement Action**.

The first two requirements may be satisfied by the **same Action**.

Roll:

**1D10 + Key Attribute**

Usually **WIL or REF**.

Add your **net successes** to the Difficulty of the next attack made against you.

Narratively, this represents spending the round watching everyone's movements — diving behind Cover when somebody begins aiming at you, sidestepping a strike, or generally taking the radical tactical approach of **not wanting to get fucking hit**.

## End of Combat

Combat ends once either side:

* Disengages.
* Is completely eliminated.
* Or no longer has any intention or capability to continue fighting.

Every character currently on the field still has their **next Action consume one full round's worth of time**.

For things such as Battlefield First Aid, once that Action finishes, time returns to normal narrative flow and the story continues as usual.
